PART THREE. WHY HELP REQUESTS ARE THE MOST VALUABLE FEEDBACK YOU HAVE.

That's a completely different quality of information. Feature requests are filtered through a person's theory of what software should do. Here's the way I'd put it. Every help request is a usability test you didn't have to run and didn't have to pay for. That's the part that should really get your attention. Every person who writes in is standing in for some number of people who hit the same wall and said nothing. So the email escape hatch isn't just a slightly worse channel. It's a data leak. Every help request that goes to email is a data point that never enters your record.

And here's what makes it worse than an ordinary leak. It's not leaking a random sample. It's leaking exactly the highest value segment. The people who are stuck. The friction. The stuff I just spent five minutes telling you is the best information you'll ever get.

Under your plan, both doors lead to the same list. So if somebody taps Feedback and then describes a problem they need help with, nothing bad has happened. The submission is in the right place. That's a genuinely good property and it's the kind of thing that decides whether a system survives contact with real users. Any design that requires people to classify themselves correctly will fail. Yours doesn't require it, it just benefits from it.

PART NINE. HOW THIS ACTUALLY WORKS ON THE BACK END.
